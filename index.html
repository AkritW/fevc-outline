8Hands-on15Hands-on: the fund with one winnerPen and paper or a calculator, in random groups of 3. A made-up fund puts $1M into each of 20 startups. Outcomes: 10 return nothing, 5 return 0.5×, 3 return 2×, 1 returns 5× and 1 returns 50×. Questions: (1) what the fund returns in total, and its multiple; (2) its multiple without the best deal; (3) what share of the returns comes from that one deal. Answers: $63.5M back, 3.2×; without the best deal $13.5M, 0.68×, so the fund loses money; one deal brings 79% of the returns. 8 (alt)Hands-on (alternative)25Hands-on: simulate the power lawIn random groups of 3, one laptop each, in Google Sheets or Excel. Each of a fund's 20 equal deals returns =IF(RAND()<0.5,0,0.5*RAND()^(-1/1.2)) times the money in (half fail; the rest follow a power law). Students simulate 1,000 funds and answer: (1) the average and the median fund multiple; (2) how often the fund returns less than the money in, and how often it triples it; (3) what share of the returns comes from the best deal; (4) the same with 10 and with 50 deals. Answer key (200,000 runs): with 20 deals the average is about 1.4× but the median only about 0.75×; about 2 in 3 funds lose money and about 1 in 20 triples; the best deal brings about a third of all returns; more deals make losing less likely (10 deals: 71%, 50 deals: 62%). Their 1,000-run answers land close to these.Thiel, Zero to One, ch. 7; course design

12Take-home15Take-home build 1, and how hidden tests grade itDue any time before the next class. They build it in any language with any tools on their own machines, deploy it to a host of their choice (the course recommends Render: free, no card, deploys from a Dockerfile, and keeping posts in memory works there; serverless hosts such as Vercel are not recommended, as in-memory data doesn't survive between requests; see sources/free-api-hosts.md), and hand in its live URL (the user, 2026-10-27). 5EngineeringData & AI1Talk15DatabasesWhy apps keep their data in a database rather than in files. Relational (SQL) databases such as PostgreSQL: tables, keys and joins, and transactions, which make a transfer between two accounts all-or-nothing (ACID); every language can talk to them. NoSQL: document stores (MongoDB), key-value stores (Redis, for caches and sessions) and wide-column stores (Cassandra), which give up some of that for scale or flexible shapes. Graph databases (Neo4j and its query language Cypher): data as nodes and edges, for questions such as 'which accounts share a phone number with a known fraudster'. Vector search (pgvector in PostgreSQL) comes back in the RAG talk. How to pick: start with PostgreSQL unless there's a reason not toPostgreSQL docs; MongoDB, Redis, Cassandra and Neo4j docs

4Talk15RAG and agentic RAGWhy a model alone gets changing facts wrong; retrieval-augmented generation: split documents into chunks, embed them, search by meaning, answer only from what was found and cite it (example: questions over Bank of Thailand rules or a bank's fee schedule). The same model does far better or worse in a different harness. MCP, the standard way to plug tools and data into any agent.
